The Assetto Corsa Competizione Porsche 911 GT3 R (covering both 991 and 992 generations) is highly setup-sensitive, with the aggressive baseline recommended as the primary starting point over the slow "safe" preset.  Key tuning parameters include maintaining 27.5 PSI for dry tires, setting TC and ABS to 3, and adjusting rear suspension stiffness and aero balance to manage the car's rear-weight bias and oversteer tendencies. 

Aero and Balance Drivers should prioritize rear downforce to stabilize the rear end, often using max rear wing on twisty tracks and minimal wing on high-speed circuits like Monza.  Front splitter adjustments should be used cautiously, as each click significantly reduces top speed; instead, aero balance is often managed by adjusting the rear wing or using negative toe (-0.3 front/rear) to improve rotation without sacrificing straight-line speed. 

Mechanical Setup The 991 GT3 R requires stiffer rear suspension relative to the front and careful management of anti-roll bars to prevent oversteer.  For the 992 GT3 R, soft springs (1-2 clicks from minimum) combined with low rear bumpstops are effective for reducing on-throttle understeer, while ride height adjustments (rake) are used to dial in balance between stability and rotation.  Electronic aids like TC2 can be increased to 4-5 for race durability, and Engine Map 8 is standard for maximum linear power in the 992. 


Key Tuning Adjustments To optimize the M4 GT3's handling, particularly to eliminate understeer, common adjustments include:

Rear Wing: Often set to maximum (full wing) for stability, though some drivers reduce it slightly on tracks requiring top speed. 
Rake: Increasing rear ride height relative to the front is critical for turning the car; the front should be as low as possible. 
Electronics: Traction Control (TC) is typically set between 2 and 4 depending on skill and track, with ABS often set to 2 or 3 to minimize braking distance without locking tires. 
Suspension: Using full soft springs, minimum differential preload, maximum negative camber, and caster helps improve cornering response.

Common Configuration Adjustments
Users frequently modify default settings to address the car's characteristic tendencies, such as understeer or rear instability. Key adjustment areas include:

Brake Bias: Moving bias rearward (e.g., to 57.8% - 59.4%) helps reduce understeer caused by the default forward-heavy bias. 
Aerodynamics: Adjusting the rear wing (often setting it to level 4 or 5) and front aero variations (sometimes negative) balances cornering speed and straight-line stability. 
Suspension: Tweaking anti-roll bars (e.g., Front 3, Rear 1) and wheel rates helps manage mechanical grip and rear-end slip during braking and turn-in. 
Electronics: Setting ABS between 3-5 and TC1 between 3-5 (with TC2 off) is a common baseline for managing traction and stability.


For the Porsche 911 RSR in Assetto Corsa Competizione (ACC), setup options are primarily categorized by weather conditions (dry vs. wet) and track characteristics (high-speed vs. bumpy/technical).  While specific numerical setups vary by track, the following guidelines outline the key adjustment areas for optimal performance. 

Dry Weather Setup Strategy

Tyre Pressures: Target a cold PSI of 27.5 for dry tyres.  Adjust slightly higher for night racing or lower for endurance races to maintain optimal temperatures.
Aerodynamics: Use minimal rear wing for high-speed tracks like Monza (0-2 clicks) to maximize straight-line speed, but increase downforce (up to full wing) for twisty tracks like Spa or Zolder to stabilize the rear-engined chassis. 
Suspension: Employ soft springs (1-2 clicks from minimum) with a higher rear ride height (rake) to improve mechanical grip and rotation. For bumpy tracks, increase ride height clearance to avoid bottoming out on kerbs. 
Electronics: Set ABS between 2-5 and Traction Control (TC) between 1-3. Lower ABS settings provide more performance but require higher skill, while TC helps preserve tyre life. 
Wet Weather Setup Strategy

Tyre Pressures: Reduce pressures to approximately 26.0 Front / 25.0 Rear to increase the contact patch for better grip on slippery surfaces. 
Aerodynamics: Increase rear wing angle (e.g., to 9°) to enhance downforce and traction.  Raise the front splitter slightly to prevent nose dive and excessive understeer.
Suspension: Soften anti-roll bars (e.g., 4 Front / 5 Rear) to improve mechanical grip and compliance with the wet track surface. 
Brakes: Adjust brake bias rearward (e.g., 50% Rear) to reduce the risk of front lockups and spins during braking. 
General Porsche 911 Setup Principles

Baseline: Start with the preset “Aggressive” setup, which offers lower TC/ABS and better ride height, then tweak for stability. 
Brake Sets: Use Brake Set 1 for hot laps, Set 2 for races under 2 hours, and Set 3 for longer endurance events to manage brake temperature and wear.
Fuel Load: Adjust fuel loads based on race length; lighter loads may require lowering the rear ride height to maintain balance. 
Handling Characteristic: The Porsche is prone to snap oversteer and instability in mid-to-high speed corners.  Use rear ride height and aero settings to balance the car, prioritizing rear stability without sacrificing too much straight-line speed.
